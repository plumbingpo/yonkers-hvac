No two heating systems break the same way, and in Yonkers homes the first question on any no-heat call is what kind of equipment we are dealing with. Gas furnaces most often stall on an oxidized flame sensor. The burners fire, the sensor fails to confirm the flame, and after three attempts the control board closes the gas valve and locks the system out. Cracked hot surface igniters, sticking pressure switches, plugged condensate traps on high-efficiency units, and tired blower motors round out the usual list. Heat pumps have their own failure patterns. A defrost cycle that underperforms lets ice build up on the outdoor coil, a bad reversing valve traps the system in cooling mode, and a dead outdoor unit hands the whole load to the electric backup strips, which cost several times as much to run.

Warning Signs for Heating Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the fix. Watch for these telltale signs:

  • Uneven heat, with certain rooms that never warm up
  • Strange smells from the vents, especially rotten egg or burning
  • Clicking and failed ignition attempts before the furnace gives up
  • Higher heating bills with less comfort to show for them
  • The system starting and stopping every few minutes without warming the house
  • No warm air at the registers even with the heat turned up
  • Banging at startup caused by burners lighting late
  • A heat pump's outdoor unit encased in frost or ice

Done Right

Why Professional Heating Repair Matters

Heating repair carries the highest stakes in home comfort work because combustion is involved. A furnace that lights late booms gas vapor inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal; a heat exchanger that cracks can pass combustion gases into the supply air; a flue that has stopped drafting sends carbon monoxide into the house instead of out of it, and none of these announce themselves in ways an untrained eye can catch. The diagnostic terrain is genuinely tricky too: a furnace that short-cycles might have a failing limit switch, or it might be protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a flame sensor without asking why it fouled leaves the next lockout already scheduled.

Our experienced technicians bring instruments and judgment to both problems. Flame signal is measured, not assumed; heat exchangers are inspected; draft is verified at the flue; temperature rise is checked against the data plate so the blower speed matches the fire. On heat pumps we test defrost boards, sensors, and reversing valves rather than condemning the compressor on a hunch, and we check whether the backup heat strips have been quietly running your bill up for weeks. The repair you get is one that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, which is exactly what you want from anyone touching the machine that keeps your family warm.

Homeowner Tips

How to Prevent Heating Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Leave open space around the furnace so the burners can draw enough combustion air
  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
  • Install a CO detector on each floor and test them every month
  • Swap the filter every month while the heat is running, since restricted airflow trips limits and shortens heat exchanger life
